MINISTRY OF PUBLIC HEALTH
Annexes A-E
National Policy on Cardiac-Arrest Readiness, Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Public Access Defibrillation
Annex A
High-Risk Places and Minimum Cardiac-Arrest Readiness Requirements
Part 1 — Covered facilities and places
The requirements of the National Policy on Cardiac-Arrest Readiness, Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Public Access Defibrillation apply to:
1.  Gyms, fitness centers, sports clubs, stadiums, athletic facilities, swimming pools and aquatic facilities.
2.  Schools, universities and educational campuses, with AED requirements implemented according to the phased schedule approved by the Ministry of Public Health.
3.  Airports, passenger ports, central transport terminals, shopping malls and public venues above a capacity threshold established by the Ministry of Public Health.
4.  Remote or difficult-access facilities where access by an AED-equipped EMS unit may be delayed.
5.  Any fixed public, educational, occupational, sporting or commercial facility where a cardiac arrest has been reported by an EMS or designated first-response agency and confirmed by the Ministry of Public Health.
6.  Any other facility designated by the Ministry of Public Health based on its activity, occupancy, population served, accessibility or emergency-response conditions.

Part 3 — Minimum facility requirements
Each covered facility shall:
1.  Designate one responsible facility contact for cardiac-emergency readiness.
2.  Maintain a Cardiac Emergency Response Plan using Annex B.
3.  Ensure that personnel trained in CPR and AED use are available during operating hours.
4.  Provide an AED where required under this Annex.
5.  Ensure that each required AED is:
operational;
accessible during operating hours;
clearly visible or identified by appropriate signage;
maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions; and
registered with the Ministry using Annex C or the approved electronic system, with geospatial linkage to the registered facility and, where appropriate, a separate AED map position.
6.  Maintain clear procedures for:
contacting EMS;
starting CPR;
retrieving and applying the AED;
ensuring EMS personnel can be promptly directed to the patient; and
transferring care.
7.  Conduct at least one practical cardiac-emergency drill annually.
8.  Submit Annex D following any suspected cardiac arrest, CPR attempt or AED application.
9.  Correct identified readiness deficiencies and restore any AED used during an emergency to operational service without unnecessary delay.
An AED shall not be considered accessible where retrieval is routinely delayed by locked access, unavailable personnel or other avoidable barriers.
Part 4 — Implementation
The Ministry of Public Health may establish:
phased implementation schedules;
public-venue capacity thresholds;
additional requirements for specific facility categories;
electronic registration, geolocation, GIS mapping and reporting procedures; and
corrective-action timelines.

3. Immediate response procedure
In a suspected cardiac arrest:
1.  Confirm that the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally.
2.  Contact EMS immediately.
3.  Start CPR.
4.  Retrieve and apply the AED.
5.  Follow the AED instructions and continue CPR.
6.  Ensure EMS personnel are met or directed promptly to the patient.
7.  Continue care until responsibility is transferred to EMS or another appropriate medical service.
8.  Complete the required incident report.

Annex E
Minimum Requirements for BLS Agencies and Designated Medical First-Response Units
These requirements apply to BLS ambulances and other designated medical first-response units responding to suspected cardiac arrest.
Part 1 — Minimum Operational Requirements
1. Equipment
Each responding unit shall carry:
1.  A functioning AED or other defibrillator authorized for use by the responding personnel.
2.  Adult electrode pads.
3.  Pediatric pads or pediatric operating capability where children may reasonably be treated.
4.  A bag-valve-mask or other approved basic ventilation equipment.
5.  Basic protective equipment required for resuscitation.
2. Personnel competence
Responding personnel shall be competent in:
1.  Recognizing suspected cardiac arrest.
2.  Performing high-quality CPR.
3.  Applying and operating the AED or authorized defibrillator.
4.  Providing basic ventilation.
5.  Working within an organized resuscitation team.
6.  Transferring care to another EMS unit or receiving emergency department.
7.  Documenting and reporting the response.
3. Operational readiness
The agency or unit shall:
1.  Ensure that the AED or authorized defibrillator and essential resuscitation equipment are operational and available whenever the unit is in service.
2.  Maintain devices and required supplies in accordance with manufacturer instructions and agency procedures.
3.  Address any unavailable or out-of-service critical equipment without unnecessary delay.
4.  Restore the unit to operational readiness after each response.
4. Cardiac-arrest response
The agency or unit shall maintain a written cardiac-arrest response procedure that includes:
1.  Confirming suspected cardiac arrest.
2.  Initiating CPR.
3.  Applying the AED or defibrillator as soon as possible.
4.  Continuing resuscitation according to the responder’s authorized scope of practice.
5.  Coordinating transport or transfer of care.
6.  Completing the required report.
Where the responding unit does not transport patients, it shall coordinate handover to the transporting EMS agency or another appropriate medical service.
5. Reporting requirement
Every attended suspected cardiac arrest shall be documented and reported through the mechanism approved by the Ministry of Public Health.
The report shall:
1.  Include the minimum dataset listed in Part 2.
2.  Identify the facility or location where the cardiac arrest occurred.
3.  Be submitted within the timeframe established by the Ministry.
4.  Not include the patient’s name in the national minimum dataset unless required through a separately authorized reporting process.
An agency may use its existing paper or electronic patient-care report where it captures the required national minimum dataset.
Part 2 — Minimum Cardiac-Arrest Reporting Dataset
One report shall be completed for each patient.
