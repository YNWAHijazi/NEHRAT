LAYOUT

Intake/details and assessment stay straightforward. After creation, use one event or venue record page.

Header: English/Arabic name, record ID, dates/routine-session context, level, deadline and current status. Keep progress visible.

Top: collapsible Required summary on the left and Recommended summary on the right. Stack on phones. Each row shows item, state and who handles it.

Below: full-width Required group, then Recommended group, each with individual collapsible cards. A summary click opens the matching card and its parents and scrolls/focuses it. No left/right tab hunt.

Bottom: final review, remaining required items with jump links, optional choices, applicable organizer declaration and Submit. After filing, the same page becomes a readable submitted snapshot with status/certificate/report actions.



RULES AND FIELDS

Companion workbook: Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 contain every revised Annex B row, quoted level rule, source responsibility, proposed editor, exact short form and completion/evidence test. Separate rows cover risk assessment, map, deployment map, organizer declaration and serious-incident notice.

The source matrix has 19 rows, not 20. Retain stable stored keys. Do not use row position as a database identifier.

Level 1 source baseline: 8 readiness/admin rows, recommended AED, conditional clinical records/reporting. No mandatory EMS invitation, plan or proof uploads. First aid and basic supplies remain required; oxygen is not a supplied universal Level 1 minimum.

Level 2 source baseline: 11 readiness/admin rows; treatment post and full plan recommended; site map and applicable organizer declaration remain package items. Owner confirmed Director recommended at Level 2 and full plan optional unless requested. Do not impose a mandatory Director or full-plan gate. D3 records the older full-MCI conflict.

Level 3: full medical plan and named Director approval. EMS or Director drafts one shared plan. Requirement answers defined in the plan link to that same data. Each EMS agency signs its own readiness declaration.

Responsibility is not the same as who types. For shared practical arrangements, the organizer or authorized participating provider can complete the single record; first valid completion counts once. Keep professional declarations, Director approval and organizer certification role-specific.



MEDICAL PLAN

Implement the 16 Protocol section 6 content areas in short collapsible sections, combining overlapping content where clearer. Reuse registration details, invitations, staffing, locations, supplies, access, communications and hospital answers. Never demand these twice.

Medical plan sheet lists all 16 areas, all 11 Level 3 major-incident elements and all 10 Level 3 per-agency declaration items. An uploaded file is not proof that every plan section is complete. The rule/evidence model must support structured section answers and actual reviewed evidence.

Director approval binds to current plan/assessment version. A material edit invalidates dependent approval. Keep prior versions and stop stale simultaneous writes from overwriting newer answers.

VENUE DIFFERENCES

Eligibility: Regularly hosts organized events and approved/licensed capacity at least 1,000. Use existing initial details and assessment. Do not infer applicability from a generic venue name. Source: Annex A Part D, Recurring Event Venues.

Assessment period: One routine operating session, not 365 days of operation. Describe usual activity, attendance, timing and access. Keep the annual assessment context visible. Source: Annex A Part B Domain 4.

Level: Derive from answers and applicable minimum conditions. Do not copy an event level to a venue or add a universal Level 2 floor. Nightclub/dance venue minimum applies where specified. Source: Annex A Parts D–E.

Requirements: Same level-based medical requirements, interpreted for routine venue operations. Use the same requirement renderer and completion rules. Replace event date/coverage context with routine sessions. Source: Protocol 2; Annex A annual assessment; Revised B.

Level 1: Organizer may complete routine first-aid, EMS contact, supplies, access, communications and escalation. No mandatory EMS/Director invite just to clear a clinical-records row. AED remains recommended. Source: Revised B A–D; latest owner request.

Shared roles: Organizer or authorized provider can record shared practical arrangements. One answer per requirement; accepted team identity reused. Professional declarations and Director approval remain personal. Source: Revised B responsibility column; owner instructions.

Medical plan: Level-based plan with shared operational answers. Do not require independent text answers plus a conflicting plan attachment. One canonical plan record and export. Source: Revised B C; Protocol 6; D2/D4.

Page layout: Same status rail, required/recommended summaries and full-width cards as event page. Single venue record page. Keep a compact details/assessment summary with deliberate edit actions where allowed. Source: Owner request; proposed UX.

Locking: Submitted/accepted package is an immutable snapshot. Allow authorized revision, never silent overwrite. Keep earlier answers/certificates accessible. Source: Product specification audit/version requirements; owner instruction.

Renewal: Annual reassessment; earlier reassessment after material changes. Same venue ID, new assessment/submission version and new certificate; retain prior certificates. Exact renewal window and validity start need D7. Source: Annex A recurring venues; owner instruction.

Material changes: Capacity, layout, crowd configuration, activity or patient access changes trigger reassessment. Recalculate level and flag affected answers/approvals. Do not silently clear all evidence or automatically approve the revision. Source: Annex A Recurring Event Venues.

Maps and declarations: Source package language is event-based; venue equivalents need an explicit mapping. Propose one permanent site map and routine deployment layout where applicable. Do not invent a route map for a building. Confirm the venue filing package before enforcing new uploads. Source: Protocol 8; Annex A; proposed venue adaptation.

After-event reports: No supplied annual venue aggregate reporting rule. Keep reports for actual events/incidents. Do not invent a mandatory annual Annex D solely because venue registration renews. Source: Protocol 13; Annex D; D8.

PAD facility service: Separate scope and readiness instrument. A venue may also be a covered PAD facility. Reuse accessible valid resources where permitted, but retain separate applicability and approvals. Source: 06 PAD specification, relationship between instruments.



SOURCE / SCOPE LIMITS

Practical fields are proposals for partner confirmation, not new clinical standards. Guidance is non-binding. No universal kit quantities, ratios or response-time pass/fail rules may be invented. The separate PAD service must keep its own applicability.

Do not call the whole application launch-ready based on these changes. Real email/OTP, hosting/data migration and backup decisions need their own verified readiness checks. They are outside this workflow handoff.
